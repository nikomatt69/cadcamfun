Read the machining side of the project. Nothing here changes an operation or a program.

Actions
- machine: the axes, rotation, zeros and accepted operations of a mill, a lathe or a 3D printer. Call it before programming.
- operations: the operations of the active drawing with their tool, depth, stepdown, feedrate and whether G-code exists.
- suggest: depth, stepdown, cutting speed, chip load and warnings for the drawing, from the parameters you give.
- compare_setups: generate and verify up to four candidate setups against the configured stock and compare them.
- analyze: static checks of a G-code program (travel limits, rapids below Z0, missing spindle or feed) plus the axis checks of the machine type.
- stock: the configured stock, its volume and whether the workpiece element matches it.
- vision: the real CAM preview with its operation and verification context.
- chain_suggest: the next operation recommended from the current chain.
- chain_status: one operation's previous operation, rest-tool reference and warnings.

Parameters (mm, mm/min, rpm)
- machine: machineType (mill|lathe|3dprinter).
- operations, stock: none.
- suggest: params - the generator settings you already know (operationType, toolDiameter, depth, stepdown, feedrate, rpm, material, offset, workOffset, ...).
- compare_setups: candidates - 1 to 4 of {name, params, toolId?, materialId?} from the libraries.
- analyze: gcode - the program text; machineType to add the axis checks.
- vision: focus - what to look at, in one line.
- chain_suggest: fromOperationId, materialHint, toolHint.
- chain_status: operationId.

Result (JSON)
- ok; machine gives the limits table; operations the list; suggest the numbers and warnings; analyze the issues (an empty list means "no static findings", never "safe"); chain_status the warnings.

Do not use it
- To program or verify: cam_edit (create, update, generate, verify, stock_set) writes.
- To read geometry: cad describes what is being machined.

After it
- Take the numbers to cam_edit, then analyse and verify the program you generated.
